On Airbnb and VRBO, guests choose from a grid of thumbnails. Before anyone reads your description, checks your reviews or compares your price, they decide in about a second whether your listing is worth a tap.

If your rental on Siesta Key, Anna Maria Island or anywhere around Sarasota is getting views but not bookings, or not getting views at all, start here.

## 1. Your cover photo is the whole ad

The first photo does most of the work. It has to say what makes the place special, and it has to read clearly as a small thumbnail on a phone.

What tends to work as a cover:

- **The pool or lanai.** In Florida, that's often the reason someone books.
- **An aerial that shows the water.** If you're a short walk from the beach or on a canal, a drone shot proves it in a way words can't.
- **A twilight exterior.** A lit pool and glowing windows stand out in a grid of midday photos.

What usually doesn't work: a dark living room, a close-up of a decor item, or the front door.

## 2. Show whole rooms, not corners

Guests are answering practical questions. Where will everyone sleep? Is the kitchen big enough to cook in? Is there somewhere to sit outside?

Photos that show the full room, taken from a corner at a natural height with straight vertical lines, answer those questions. Tight, artful crops look nice but leave guests guessing. Guests who are guessing keep scrolling.

## 3. Order the gallery the way a guest walks through

A good gallery tells a story:

1. The cover, your strongest shot
2. Main living area
3. Kitchen and dining
4. Primary bedroom, then the other bedrooms
5. Bathrooms
6. Outdoor space: pool, lanai, yard
7. Aerials and the neighborhood: the beach, the walk, the view
8. The extras: laundry, beach gear, parking

![Aerial view of a Southwest Florida home and its neighborhood](images/aerial/DJI_0970-Enhanced-NR.jpg "An aerial answers the first question every guest asks: what's around it?")

## 4. Add a floor plan

For families and groups, sleeping arrangements decide the booking. A simple 2D floor plan answers "who sleeps where?" at a glance, and cuts down on the questions you'd otherwise answer by message.

## 5. Use video where it earns attention

A short vertical reel works on your Instagram and Facebook, on a direct-booking site, and anywhere you share the listing link. It shows the flow of the house in a way still photos can't.

## 6. Get the house camera-ready

No edit hides clutter. Before a shoot:

- Clear counters, nightstands and bathroom vanities
- Make the beds tightly with crisp white or neutral linens
- Turn on every light and open the blinds
- Put the pool cover away and skim the pool
- Move cars, bins and hoses out of the exterior shots

Every client gets a pre-shoot checklist after booking for exactly this reason.

## 7. Refresh your photos when the property changes

New furniture, a renovated kitchen, a heated pool. If it isn't in the photos, guests don't know it exists. New photos are also a good moment to revisit your title and description before season.

> Guests book the feeling. The photos are where they find it.
